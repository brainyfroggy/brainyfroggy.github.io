About me

I study how AI can help us understand the brain, and how neuroscience can inform better AI.

I’m Yujun (Eugene) Chen, a Ph.D. candidate in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Florida, working in the Ding Laboratory. My research explores how the brain represents emotional and social information across space and time.

Research direction

I’m interested in building shared models of how the brain responds to images, video, and language. By bringing together fMRI, EEG, eye-tracking, and behavioral data in a common representational space—and accounting for differences between people and experiments—these models could connect findings across studies.

I want to use such models to generate synthetic neural responses when measured data are scarce and to test competing neuroscience hypotheses before designing new experiments. For example, model ablations could help identify which visual pathways support emotion and social understanding. Predictions and improvements would be tested against held-out human data, with targeted experiments feeding back into the models.

The exchange goes both ways: I’m also interested in using findings about neural representations, dynamics, and feedback to develop more capable AI systems.
